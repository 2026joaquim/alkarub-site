The story of Chai: from the ships to India to Algarve carob

Published on November 6, 2026 · Alkarub

Chai was born from spices that travelled the routes between India and the world. Alkarub Chai brings that journey back to the Algarve, where the ships set out from, and adds carob, one of the oldest trees of our land.

Chai: one word, one journey

“Chai” simply means “tea” in several languages of the Indian subcontinent. Masala chai is a warm, aromatic spiced drink, traditionally enjoyed with milk. It is a drink for conversation and hospitality.

The Algarve, a land of departures

The spices that shaped chai reached Europe along routes that Portuguese navigators helped to open. The Algarve, with Lagos and Sagres, was a starting point and a place of preparation for those voyages.

It is said that the ships also carried carob, a food that keeps well and gives energy, for the sailors and for the animals on board. Carob left the Algarve long before it became our chai.

Where carob meets the spices

Alkarub Chai joins the two ends of that story. On one side, Algarve carob, naturally sweet, with notes of cocoa and a velvety taste. On the other, the spices of the Spice Route, which bring warmth and aroma.

There are 8 ingredients in all, blended by hand in Budens: Algarve carob (65%), cinnamon, ginger, cardamom, fennel, clove, black pepper and star anise.

A gentle chai, without exaggeration

Carob has no caffeine or theine and needs no sugar to be sweet, and the spices give it character. With milk, the drink becomes even creamier and more velvety. Spices have been used for centuries for their aroma and in the wellbeing traditions of many cultures, and that is how we present them, as a pleasure and a habit, without promising cures.

How to drink it

You can make it with cow's milk, milk from other animals or a plant-based drink. Simmer 1 tea bag in 100 ml of water for 5 minutes, add 200 ml of milk or plant-based drink and heat. Squeeze the bag and serve, sweetened to taste.
